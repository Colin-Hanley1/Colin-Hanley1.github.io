Selected work

  • 2026

    College Basketball Predictive Model

    An analytics platform for 5,000+ Division I players and 360+ teams. Opponent-adjusted metrics (BPM, VORP, composite ratings) fed by automated scraping pipelines with fuzzy entity resolution; delivered as an interactive web application.

  • 2026

    Markovian Inning Model

    First-inning run scoring modeled as a 24-state absorbing Markov chain. NRFI probabilities computed both analytically, via fundamental matrix inversion, and empirically, via 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations with confidence intervals. Automated daily pipeline.

  • 2025

    Groundwater Modeling Research

    With Dr. Jia Zhao at the University of Alabama Department of Mathematics. A Transformer architecture with Multiresolution Hash Encoding trained on two decades of cleaned US groundwater data. ASSURE grant awardee.

  • 2025

    Poacher Detection in Infrared

    Fine-tuned a custom YOLOv5 detector for real-time identification of human presence in LWIR UAV imagery. Achieved 76 percent recall on the targeted class through a pseudo-labeled synthetic data pipeline. Published as an academic paper.

  • 2025

    Agent-Based COVID-19 Simulator

    A Mesa-based agent simulation of outbreak dynamics with scenario testing for vaccine strategies, masking policy, and lockdown thresholds. Features household and workplace contact structures, age-based risk profiles, and dynamic intervention triggers.

  • 2024

    Genetic Fantasy Football Drafter

    A genetic algorithm for draft strategy optimization, packaged as a Dash application used live on draft night.

About

I am a Mathematics and Data Science student at the University of Alabama, where I hold a 3.97 grade point average and will graduate in May 2027. Since January 2025 I have worked as an undergraduate research assistant under Dr. Jia Zhao in the Department of Mathematics, building a Transformer-based model for groundwater dynamics using Multiresolution Hash Encoding.

My personal projects sit at the intersection of applied machine learning and analytics, most often in sports, healthcare informatics, and computer vision. I am drawn to problems where careful modeling, honest evaluation, and clear communication all matter.

Before college I spent four winters in Montana as a PSIA-certified snowsports instructor, which taught me how to teach. I bring that same approach to data work — explain the problem, show the work, and leave the audience more capable than you found them.
